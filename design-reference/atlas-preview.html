The Cartographer's Desk

Three decisions are finalised. Capture them here so nothing is relitigated downstream.

  • Land & MagicThe land is the map; magic is a quiet, opt-in layer. Switch it off and you have an aged atlas. Switch it on and the Winds of Magic bloom over the terrain.
  • No AffinityMagic has no type per location. Mana is one fuel — like the Winds of Magic in Total War: Warhammer — that drifts and pools across the whole world. There are wells (concentrated founts) and dead-calm zones, nothing more.
  • Percentile BiomesBiome thresholds are percentile-based on landmass hypsometry, not absolute elevation cutoffs. Every world has realistic proportions of highlands versus lowlands regardless of seed.

V · The Winds

The Winds of Magic

One mana field. No affinity. The wind blows where it will — gathering into wells, fading into dead-calm zones.

The Model

Mana is a single fuel that drifts across the entire world — sea and land alike. It gathers into wells (concentrated founts) and disperses into dead-calm zones. The terrain only lightly stirs it: rugged ground causes a gust, deep ocean a slight lull. There are no types, no schools, no affinities at the geographic level.

This is intentional. The schools of the Arcana impose human categories on magic; the world itself does not. A mage of Evocation draws from the same wind as a mage of Enchantment — only their art differs.

The Two Modes

Wells — three concentric rings mark the strongest pockets of the wind. Useful for worldbuilding: where would mages build academies? Where do ley lines converge?

Winds — the full mana field as a violet wash over the terrain, intensity = opacity. Shows how the wind fills the world — not just the peaks but the broad, soft pockets between.

A well — three rings scaled by intensity, the bright core the strongest point of the wind's gathering.
